# OOD Detection Scope — Step 13

## What was tested

**Material shift (Tier A synthetic maps — fully tested)**

All tests ran on 64×64 synthetic Raman maps generated by `MapSimulator` using the
`configs/simulator.yaml` `materials` block. Four OOD cases were evaluated:

| Case | Material | OOD axis | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| OOD-A | `amorphous_silicon` | Broad band (FWHM 70 cm⁻¹) | Breaks the linewidth prior (3.5 cm⁻¹) |
| OOD-B | `sic_4h` | Two modes at 776 + 796 cm⁻¹ | Single-Lorentzian structurally wrong |
| OOD-C | `gan` | Band at 567 cm⁻¹ | Far from 520.7 cm⁻¹ window anchor |
| OOD-D | `si_doublet` | Two modes at 519.7 + 521.7 cm⁻¹ | **Hard case** — fits as one broad peak |

The in-distribution null was calibrated on silicon maps (indices 0–5), scored on held-out silicon (indices 6–8). Null FPR target: 5%.

Three detectors were compared:
- `score_residual_structure` — combined χ²ᵥ (upper tail) + lag-1 autocorrelation
- `score_parameter_plausibility` — FWHM and center prior deviations
- `score_embedding_distance` — PCA Mahalanobis baseline

## What was deferred and why

### 1. Excitation wavelength shift
**Deferred to Tier B.**

Detecting a change in excitation wavelength (e.g. 532 nm → 785 nm) requires
modelling wavelength-dependent Raman cross-sections and fluorescence backgrounds.
The Tier A simulator has no wavelength dimension. Tier B hardware data is required
to characterise the instrument's spectral response at multiple wavelengths before
this axis can be tested rigorously.

### 2. Grating / dispersion change
**Deferred — cheap to add, not tested now.**

A different grating changes the dispersion (cm⁻¹/px), which shifts the peak position
on the detector grid and alters the effective FWHM in pixels. The detector would need
a second instrument profile in `configs/simulator.yaml`. This is straightforward to
add but requires a second calibrated dispersion value from hardware (Step 15). Adding
a synthetic second dispersion without hardware grounding would be testing the simulator,
not the instrument.

### 3. Instrument unit (different physical spectrometer)
**Deferred to Step 15 (Hardware Validation).**

Unit-to-unit variability (different slit widths, alignment, detector QE curves,
CCD columns) cannot be characterised without data from at least two physical
instruments. This is the most important axis for commercial deployment but is
entirely hardware-gated. It will be addressed in Step 15 using real HORIBA spectra.
